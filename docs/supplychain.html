The Earth–Moon supply chain

Everything on the Moon today was launched from Earth. Proposed supply chains would change that, with fuel stops in space, parking spots at Lagrange points, and Moon resources sent back home. Pick a route, then click any object to see what it does.

    Parking spots in space

    The five Lagrange points

    Where Earth's and the Moon's pull balance out, a spacecraft can travel around Earth in step with the Moon. There are five of these spots, named L1 to L5 after the mathematician Joseph-Louis Lagrange.

    Why a fuel stop matters

    Escaping Earth costs far more than escaping the Moon

    Rocket scientists measure the effort of a trip as "delta-v": the total change in speed a rocket must make. The more delta-v, the more of the rocket has to be fuel.

    Would Moon fuel actually be cheaper?

    Studies disagree. The answer depends on how heavy and efficient Moon factories turn out to be, and how cheap launches from Earth become.

    For: industry-led study, 2018

    3×+

    Refueling with Moon propellant could cut the cost of getting from Earth to the Moon's surface by more than three times.

    Against: NASA-sponsored study, 2019

    +97%

    Under its assumptions, Moon-made propellant would cost 97% more than launching it from Earth. Extraction systems would need to be 2–3 times more efficient to break even.

    No supply chain beyond Earth exists yet. Fuel has never been moved between two large spacecraft in orbit, and nothing has been mined on the Moon. Every route on this page past "launch everything from Earth" is a proposal, a test or a plan.
