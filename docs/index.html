Everywhere in Moon soil

Oxygen is 40–45% of the soil by weight, locked in minerals. Silicon is in almost every rock. Both can be split out with electricity to make air, rocket fuel and solar panels.

Platinum-group metals may sit in old asteroid impact craters, possibly thousands of them, but no deposit has been found yet.

Helium-3

A rare gas the Sun dusted onto the Moon

Earth's magnetic field blocks the solar wind. The Moon has none, so helium-3 has built up in its soil for billions of years.

→How it got there

Water ice

Ice in craters that never see the Sun

Drinking water, air and rocket fuel, frozen at the south pole. It's why nearly every country is aiming there.

Rare earths

Magnet metals in old lava plains

Rock called KREEP, rich in rare earth elements, rings the Imbrium basin. On Earth, China refines most of the supply.
