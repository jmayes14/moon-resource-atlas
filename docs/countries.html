Who's going

Two teams, one small target

The United States and China lead two rival groups of countries. Both plan bases near the Moon's south pole in the 2030s, because that is where the ice is.

The two groups

Artemis Accords (led by the United States)

78 countries

as of 28 October 2026, when Vietnam joined. 17 countries signed in 2026 alone, the most in any year.

A set of shared, non-binding rules for exploring the Moon. They say countries and companies may use resources they collect, while still following the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which says no country can own the Moon.

International Lunar Research Station (led by China and Russia)

13 countries

as tracked in mid-2026, plus several organizations.

A plan for a shared base at the south pole, with a basic version by about 2035. Partners named in public reports include Pakistan, Azerbaijan, South Africa, Nicaragua and the United Arab Emirates. Fewer details of its rules are public.

A few countries, such as Thailand and Senegal, have signed both.

Why the south pole? It has ice in craters that never see sunlight, and nearby ridges that get sunlight almost all the time for solar power. Good landing spots there are few. The United States, China, India and Russia all plan to land within a few hundred kilometers of each other. See it on the Moon
